AI-Driven Training Simulation Design

Details of the employer, product, and internal materials are under NDA, so this describes the design work in general terms. I designed and QA-tested a natural-language training scenario for a conversational-AI character: the character's behavior logic, a graph-based conversation design that works around a common LLM limitation, a weighted scoring rubric for grading trainee performance, and a structured testing process to validate it all before handoff.

Internship AI / Conversational Design Confidential
My Role

I owned the design of the AI character and the conversation logic behind one training scenario: personality and speaking-style configuration, a topic-scoped knowledge base, and a state-machine narrative design. I then authored a weighted, multi-criteria scoring rubric for grading trainee performance, ran a structured QA pass comparing the character's actual responses against an expected-behavior spec, and documented the gap between the current proof of concept and the target production system for the rest of the team.

Team

A small cross-functional internship team: character/conversation design and QA (my focus), UI/UX design, and HR-domain research, working under a supervising manager with engineering support for the eventual production integration.

The Design Problem
1

The model can't reliably recall the conversation

Most conversational-AI platforms judge behavior turn by turn, not across the full conversation history. Any design that depends on the character "remembering" earlier turns tends to break down in practice.

2

A linear script kills replayability

Scripted branches force every playthrough down the same path. The design needed to track a trainee's progress toward specific behaviors without asking the LLM to hold that state itself.

3

Covering the right topics isn't the same as handling them well

A trainee can raise every required topic and still handle the conversation badly — or handle it well without ever naming the underlying issue. Scoring needed to separate what was said from how it was said.

Conversation Design — A State Machine, Not a Script

The character's behavior is built as a graph rather than a top-to-bottom script. States that look like duplicates exist purely to count a trainee's progress across turns without asking the model to remember anything — a workaround for the turn-by-turn judgment constraint above. Scoring — A Weighted, Multi-Criteria Rubric

Modeled on a Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scale: several weighted criteria combine into a single score, mixing topic-coverage criteria ("did the trainee raise the right issues") with behavioral-quality criteria ("did they communicate well under pressure"). One criterion acts as a hard floor — if the trainee fails a core leadership/communication behavior outright, the session fails automatically regardless of every other score, rather than letting a strong performance elsewhere average it out.

Topic coverage

Did the trainee raise the right issues?

Scored on whether specific required topics were surfaced and connected to their broader organizational impact, not just mentioned in passing.

Behavioral floor

Did the trainee hold a firm, appropriate position?

Backing down, avoiding the core issue, or capitulating under pushback is an automatic fail on this criterion — and on the whole session — independent of every other score.

Communication quality

Was the tone constructive under pressure?

Fact-based, non-accusatory framing throughout scores higher than ultimatums or reactive escalation when the other party pushes back.

Procedural correctness

Did the trainee follow the right process?

Confidentiality maintained, policy-framed rather than personal-opinion language, a specific expectation set, consequences stated where appropriate.

QA Testing

Each topic area was tested against multiple hand-written prompts — spanning a good, middling, and poor communication approach — with an expected response path defined in advance, then compared turn by turn against the character's actual output. The pass rate across the full test suite was very high; one edge case in a lower-priority topic area didn't progress as expected, which I flagged as a real gap rather than a scoring ambiguity.

Pass
Core behavioral topics

Good, middling, and poor communication approaches to the same underlying issue consistently routed to their expected response path.

Pass
Escalation & de-escalation arcs

Sustained good communication measurably softened the character over several turns; sustained poor communication reached a hard shutdown faster; a single recovery message only partially re-engaged the character rather than fully resetting the arc.

Pass
Off-topic & drift handling

A single tangent gets one in-character redirect; a second consecutive tangent correctly triggered a hard shutdown and ended the session.

Pass
Edge cases

Confirmed a deliberately-unpatched "bargaining" trap (the character softens for an incentive rather than firm policy — a real behavioral trap, not a bug), that a closing-sounding question alone doesn't end the scenario, and that the character never breaks its persona to thank the trainee.

Key Design Decisions
Adopted

Dual-AI: one plays the character, one scores it

Asking a single model to stay in character in real time and objectively grade the conversation at the same time degrades both jobs — so scoring is deferred entirely to a separate post-session evaluation pass over the transcript.

Adopted

Topic coverage removed from real-time judgment

Whether all required topics were raised is judged accurately after the fact, not asked of the in-conversation character model — which also keeps the character from steering the trainee toward "correct" phrases.

Adopted

Progress indicator shows graph state, not a topic checklist

Surfacing the actual grading criteria in the UI would recreate the exact problem the conversation design avoids: the character (or the UI) coaching the trainee on what to say.

Left unfixed, deliberately

A known "bargaining" trap

The character can be persuaded with an incentive rather than firm, policy-based leadership — a deliberately realistic weak spot to test for, not a bug. Kept as a scoring trap rather than patched, and confirmed by QA to still trigger correctly.

Adopted

Stable, non-flagship model tier

Character conversations run on a stable, non-preview model rather than anything tagged "latest" or "beta," to conserve limited usage quota and keep behavior reproducible during testing.

Adopted

Escalation modeled as one coupled dimension

Modeled as distance from a center point rather than two separate meters, so "how much patience is left" automatically depends on "how escalated the character is" without manually tuned per-level thresholds.

Outcome
Delivered
  • Character and narrative-design graph validated end to end on the conversational-AI platform
  • Weighted, multi-criteria scoring rubric with a hard behavioral floor condition
  • A structured QA suite across multiple topic areas, with a near-complete pass rate against expected behavior
  • A written architecture assessment separating the working proof of concept from the target production system
  • Progress presented to the team's stakeholders at a project check-in
